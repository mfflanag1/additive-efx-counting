# Four-owner decorated-component exhaustion lemma

**Date:** 2026-08-11

**Status:** cap-free elimination of repeated outside-component recruitment;
the full-owner base-margin/direct-safety terminal remains open

## Two physical components already use three owners

In a surplus-decorated response, the changed target owner `j` and response
source `s` are distinct and are joined by the response transfer.  Their
physical component `A` therefore satisfies

\[
 |A|\ge2.                                           \tag{1}

\]

A cross-component observer `h` belongs to a disjoint nonempty signed-transfer
component `C`, so

\[
 |A\cup C|\ge3.                                    \tag{2}

\]

For four owners, at most one owner `e` lies outside `A union C`.

## Exhaustive recruitment consequence

Apply the component-wide current/base-floor selector.

1. If its connected bridge succeeds, the physical components merge and the
   payment/observer-response continuation applies.
2. If it reaches the base-margin separator and a packet from outside
   `A union C` repairs the source or a donor floor, adjoining its supplier
   strictly grows the represented physical owner union to all four owners.
3. Once all four owners are represented, no further outside-component
   recruitment exists.  A repeated decorated response must therefore:
   - satisfy the sacrificed-source direct-safety constraints;
   - use a genuinely new response core, giving permanent response
     augmentation; or
   - return to the same full-owner decorated response and its exact base-
     margin separator.

Thus outside recruitment is a one-time progress event, not a recurrence.
No item or value bound is used; the only cardinality input is the four-owner
theorem being proved.

## Exact terminal

After owner-union saturation and response-record saturation, the remaining
state has all of the following named data:

1. the exact response core `D` and current/base source margins;
2. every observer's signed surplus transfer;
3. the complete connected observer-component pool and protected floors;
4. the global base-margin separator over every debt-removing packet and every
   redistribution of that pool; and
5. failure of the sacrificed source's final direct EFX/charity constraints.

This is the first point at which a full globally lex-maximal GG countermodel
could refute the connected-splice route.  Any proposed countermodel missing
one of these five conditions only refutes a weaker local certificate.
