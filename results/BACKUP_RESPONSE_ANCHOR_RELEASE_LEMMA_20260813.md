# Backup-response anchor-release lemma

**Date:** 2026-08-13

**Status:** superseded by
[`PERMANENT_ANCHOR_RETIRE_ON_BACKUP_EXCHANGE_THEOREM_20260813.md`](PERMANENT_ANCHOR_RETIRE_ON_BACKUP_EXCHANGE_THEOREM_20260813.md),
which removes an overstrong wall-localization sentence and proves the
uniform supplier-return operation

## Input

Let `Y` be the current post-recruitment allocation. The root owns

\[
 B_r=K\mathbin{\dot\cup}E,                       \tag{1}
\]

where `K` is the permanent old anchor and `E` is a disjoint current-safe root
response satisfying

\[
 v_r(E)>v_r(Y_r^{\mathrm{pre}})
 \ge \max\{v_r(X_r),\max_{T\ne B_r}\tau_r(T)\}.  \tag{2}
\]

Let nonroot owner `d` hold residual `W`. Suppose an anchor-side core
`A subseteq K` creates the source-inactive loss band

\[
 v_d(W)<\tau_d(A)<v_d(A)<v_d(X_d).                \tag{3}
\]

Every represented nonroot owner holds a protected core and is paid against
every current target.

## Controlled anchor release

Release the entire old anchor to the residual owner:

\[
 Z_r=E,
 \qquad Z_d=W\mathbin{\dot\cup}K,                 \tag{4}
\]

and leave every other owner and charity unchanged.

The allocation is feasible because `K,E,W` are pairwise disjoint. The root
remains strictly above the base and pays every unchanged target by (2).
Owner `d` strictly improves by positivity and hence remains safe against
every target she previously paid. The old root target shrinks from `K+E` to
`E`, so no unchanged observer can acquire a new wall against it. Every other
target is unchanged except `W`, which enlarges to `W+K`.

Consequently no genuinely new wall can occur except against `W+K`.  An old
wall of the sacrificed owner against a retained core may persist and must
keep its old label.  Subject to that correction, exactly one of the following
holds after all pre-existing walls have already been exhausted.

1. `Z` is EFX and strictly lexicographically improves `X`.
2. Some represented source strongly envies the single enlarged target
   `W+K`. Global minimum extraction gives a labeled safe desired response
   inside that target.

There are no other **new** EFX failures. In branch 2 the complete response and
signed release record is new finite augmentation or an exact one-target
recurrence handled by recapture/hole-pivot matching. The permanent anchor is
deliberately released, so the transition cannot be misclassified as a
fixed-anchor recurrence.

## Why the generic source-inactive terminal disappears

The generic root-facing repair has to keep its only root core and may end at
a root-critical least good. Here `E` is an independent paid backup. It pays
the root before any partition of `K+W` is considered, so the root-priority
constraint causing that critical branch vanishes. Giving the entire released
anchor to `d` is already the most favorable cell for the trapped row and
converts every external objection into one ordinary changed-target response.

Thus the source-inactive loss band associated with the permanent-anchor
hitting state always yields outcome 1, 3, 4, or 5 of the recurrence theorem:
EFX, a new response record, controlled anchor release, or one-target response
compression. It cannot recur while retaining the permanent anchor.

## Claim boundary

This lemma proves feasibility, root payment, strict residual improvement,
complete wall localization, and explicit anchor release. It does not by
itself terminate an exact response recurrence inside `W+K`, prove every
such recurrence is PI, close other non-anchor GG branches, or prove four-
agent/general additive EFX.
