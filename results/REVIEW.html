EFX at (4, 8): an exact-search and structure campaign on the first open cell of additive fair division

Max Flanagan's counterexample-hunt project, executed with a multi-agent Claude Fable 5 pipeline (dual verifiers, CEGAR solvers, theory agents, literature agents). Working artifacts in ~/projects/counterexample-hunt/efx/ (a public frozen archive is a pre-circulation TODO; see §10).

5 bounded counterexample-exclusion results (4 at n=4, 1 composite at n=5) 2 instances with exactly 4 EFX₀ allocations, dual-verified (4,8) open — unrestricted engine still running 2 referee rounds incorporated; all findings verified & applied

1Summary

The target is the open problem of fair division: do EFX allocations always exist for n ≥ 4 agents with additive valuations over indivisible goods? Following the 2026 wave of AI-assisted counterexamples (and, in this problem's own family, the monotone/submodular and additive-chores EFX disproofs), we ran an intensive exact-search campaign on the smallest genuinely open cell, 4 agents and 8 goods, using an exact-verifier-first discipline.

Executive claim (calibrated)

We report four computer-assisted bounded positive-integer UNSAT results for n = 4, two independently checked instances with exactly four EFX₀ allocations, and several structural hypotheses intended to guide the unresolved unrestricted (4,8) problem. A fifth bounded result at n = 5 is now complete as a three-part composite (see §4). No counterexample was found anywhere; evidence within the searched bounded and heuristic regimes is consistent with existence at n = 4 for m ≤ 10.

Calibration: nothing here is a breakthrough. The proven pieces are PhD-student-grade computational results plus incremental lemmas; the genuinely new prizes (an unconditional (4,8) resolution, or a witness at any cell) remain pending. This is revision 2.1 (two referee rounds): internal refereeing corrected one advertised theorem, a variant-coverage claim, a mislabeled empirical statistic, and two over-broad structural claims — details flagged inline and in §7.

2Problem position (verified against the literature)

EFX₀ ("envy-free up to any good"): allocation X₁…Xₙ of all goods is EFX₀ if for every pair i, j and every good g ∈ X_j: v_i(X_i) ≥ v_i(X_j) − v_i(g). Status as of July 2026:

  • Fallen neighbors (2026): EFX fails for monotone/submodular valuations, n ≥ 3, m ≥ n+5 — found by SAT solving, encoding Lean-verified (Akrami–Mayorov–Mehlhorn–Srinivas–Weidenbach, arXiv:2604.18216); compact symmetric subadditive counterexamples (Mackenzie–Suzuki, arXiv:2605.06451); EFX fails for additive chores, n ≥ 4, tri-valued costs (He–Tao, arXiv:2606.08872). Additive goods is the last tower: 2604.18216 states explicitly that even n = 4 additive is unknown.
  • Proven-safe classes: n ≤ 3 additive (Chaudhury–Garg–Mehlhorn; strongest form Akrami et al., arXiv:2205.07638); m ≤ n+3 for the strong every-good variant (Mahara, arXiv:2107.09901); ≤ 3 distinct valuation types (Vishwa Prakash H.V. et al., arXiv:2410.13580, EC 2025); bivalued instances; identical valuations. Almost-full: 4-agent additive instances always admit EFX on all but one good (Berger–Cohen–Feldman–Fiat, arXiv:2102.10654).
  • First open cell: (n, m) = (4, 8), for unrestricted additive-goods instances. Even the monotone (4,8) case is unresolved — the SAT work solved n = 3 only. No competing work on additive n = 4 appeared on arXiv July 15–23, 2026.
  • Zero values. The ε-perturbation reduction (Amanatidis et al., arXiv:2001.09838) transfers an unrestricted all-positive existence theorem to nonnegative valuations. It does not upgrade our fixed-value-cap searches (perturbing and clearing denominators leaves the searched box), so the bounded results in §4 are stated for positive integer values only. A prospective unrestricted result (the Z3 engine, §9) would cover zeros via this reduction.

3Method and verification discipline

  • Dual independent verifiers. Two exhaustive EFX₀ checkers written independently (different sessions, different enumeration algorithms), exact integer arithmetic, cross-validated on random instances plus theorem-backed self-tests; a later numpy counter validated to 100% exact agreement on 60+ instances. Every instance-level claim below passed both. Scope caveat (referee): the verifiers validate instances; they do not independently validate the CEGAR master encodings.
  • CEGAR core. Master (CP-SAT over integers; separately Z3 over exact rationals): find valuations killing all pooled allocations. Slave (exact verifier): find EFX₀ allocations of the candidate; pool them. Master infeasibility = exhaustion of the searched class; slave failure = witness. Search-space cuts: row/column double-lex (Flener et al.); a distinct-rows cut justified by the ≤3-types theorem — sound at n = 4, where it was designed; at n = 5 it silently strengthened to "5 distinct types," which is what forced the §4 downgrade.
  • Calibration. The pipeline returned the expected verdict on four finite control classes before touching the open cell: (2,3); (3,5); (4,7) [Mahara]; (4,8) restricted to two values [bivalued].
  • Directed search. Surrogate-objective annealing (minimize EFX₀ count, tie-break toward fragile survivors) plus CP-SAT finishers that exactly exhaust boxes around near-witnesses.
  • Claim hygiene. Novelty checks before claiming lemmas; numerical testing of every theory claim; an adversarial referee round on this report itself. The catches are listed in §7 — including one catch that was itself wrong and was reversed on re-audit.

5The near-witness ladder and the count-4 phenomenon

Directed annealing (in the lineage of earlier local-search approaches to EFX, cf. Brânzei, arXiv:2510.05429) drives the number of EFX₀ allocations from ~300–2,500 (random instances) down a reproducible ladder: 184 → 118 → 18 → 5 → 4. The decisive lever was a surrogate objective tie-breaking toward fragile survivors.

Empirical observation (dual-verified instances; not a proved law)

The minimum EFX₀-allocation count reached by deep directed search is exactly 4 — never 3, 2, 1, or 0 — at (4,8) across six independent basins, and at (4,9) and (4,10). Positioning against the direct prior art — Neoh–Teh (AAAI 2025), read in full: their Table 1 gives, for the minimum number of EFX allocations, a tight bound n at m = n+1, bounds [n, n²] at m = n+2, and for m ≥ n+3 only the upper bound n! with no nontrivial lower bound; their Conjecture 6.1 asserts every instance has at least n EFX allocations; their exactly-n instances are at m = n+2 with n ≤ 4, and for n = 5 they report an empirical minimum of 14. Our contribution relative to this: explicit dual-verified instances with exactly n = 4 EFX₀ allocations at m = n+4, n+5, and n+6 — improving the known upper bound on the minimum count in their m ≥ n+3 regime from n! to n, which is tight if their Conjecture 6.1 holds. (The tight set now spans three consecutive n: dual-verified instances with exactly n EFX₀ allocations at (4,7), (5,8), and (6,9) — every m = n+3 cell searched — plus n = 4 tight instances at m = 8, 9, 10. In each cell the published minimum-count upper bound was n!; these instances lower it to n, matching Conjecture 6.1's floor exactly. The (6,9) witness was constructed directly by an anatomy-constrained CEGAR whose master encodes the proven hub-plus-singletons-plus-floater structure.) We claim the instances, not a floor theorem; the floor-at-4 phenomenon is exactly their conjecture holding empirically at its boundary.

In both, the four surviving allocations are one base allocation of 7 goods plus the four placements of a low-value "floater" good — the last-good parking structure that is the known crux of the charity line (arXiv:2102.10654). Precision note (referee): neither floater is a universal minimum (basin 1: agent 1 values good 3 at 2 with row-minimum 1), so the universal-min Parking-Floor Lemma of §6 does not by itself explain these two matrices; the generalized per-observer placement condition does, base by base. The parking picture is an interpretation, not a proved characterization of these instances.

6Structural results

Proven (and re-tested during the referee round)

  • Peeling Theorem. If some agent a has a good t with v_a(t) ≥ v_a(M∖{t}) − ming≠t v_a(g), then assigning a the singleton {t} and applying the 3-agent theorem to the remaining agents and goods yields EFX₀. Consequence: a counterexample has no dominant good for any agent.
  • Five-shape reduction. In any EFX₀ allocation of 8 goods among 4 agents with positive values, all bundles are nonempty, so the size profile is one of 5111, 4211, 3311, 3221, 2222; singleton bundles are never strong-envy targets, reducing each shape to an explicit inequality system. Coverage results, with their exact hypotheses: three distinct agents holding three distinct dominant goods ⇒ 5111 feasible; the dominant-pair theorem yields a two-treasure allocation whose shape may be 5111, 4211, or 3311 (the two-agent residual split is not size-controlled; a size-controlled split lemma would be needed to pin the shape). Withdrawn (referee): "flat/near-identical ⇒ 2222" — false as stated; four identical rows [100,1,…,1] admit 2,520 EFX₀ allocations, all of shape 3221. Identical valuations are EFX-safe (leximin), but not via the 2222 shape.
  • Rotation invariance (= Mahara Lemma 7 / Lipton et al. 2004). Rotating bundles along a plain-envy cycle preserves EFX₀: movers strictly gain, the bundle multiset is fixed. Re-audited during the referee round: 0 breaks in 34,538 cycle-rotations. (See §7 for the history of this item.)
  • Parking-Floor Lemma (universal-min form). For a good f that is every agent's minimum, EFX₀ completions of an EFX₀ base of M∖{f} correspond exactly to the un-envied bundles (sources) of the base. Sufficiency is the standard charity-line argument, and Neoh–Teh's Theorem 3.3 already contains the universally-zero special case of this counting mechanism (referee-checked); ours is at most a modest extension of their machinery to the universal-minimum case.
  • Necessary conditions on any witness (selectively encoded across the exact masters — not all conditions in all masters): no dominant good; at most one agent with a good worth ≥ half its row total; no universally-minimum good; pairwise non-proportional rows; and, from the almost-full theorem, every witness contains an EFX₀ base of 7 goods whose four floater-placements all fail.

Search-guiding hypothesis (not a proved reduction)

Conjectural obstruction family (open)

All three near-witnesses are misaligned two-treasure instances with small slack in their binding EFX inequalities. We conjecture — we have not proven — that any witness must live in a misaligned multi-treasure regime; several per-shape and aligned cases in the supporting notes remain open, "zero slack" is not shown necessary, and the corridor CEGAR imposes fixed numerical bands. Status: a motivated search heuristic. Every completed bounded-band exhaustion under it returned UNSAT; stalled and terminated searches (§8) are inconclusive.

7Corrections, rediscoveries, and reversals

Full discipline record — nothing claimed externally

Rediscovery. Our "Source Lemma" (every EFX-admitting instance has an EFX allocation with acyclic envy graph) is Mahara arXiv:2008.08798 Lemma 7, rooted in Lipton–Markakis–Mossel–Saberi (EC 2004). Caught by novelty check before claiming.

A correction that was itself wrong (caught by referee round, verified by re-audit). An internal report claimed naive envy-cycle rotation breaks EFX₀ in ~46% of cases, and revision 1 of this document repeated it. The 46% statistic actually measured rotation followed by parking a leftover good; pure rotation preserved EFX₀ in 0/1,636 original tests and 0/34,538 re-audit tests, consistent with the standard proof. Revision 1's "flawed proof" entry is withdrawn; the rotation lemma stands (as Mahara's, not ours).

Downgraded theorem (referee). Revision 1 advertised a (5,9) bounded theorem; the encoding's distinct-rows cut is only justified at n = 4 and silently excluded four-type profiles at n = 5. Downgraded to partial. A first completion attempt was itself found unsound in referee round 2 (symmetry-breaking forced the duplicated type lex-max); its UNSAT covers only that subclass. A corrected run is pending; the row stays partial until it terminates.

Withdrawn claims (referee): zero-value coverage of the bounded results (§2); "flat ⇒ 2222" (§6); "the remaining problem is exactly the corridor" (§6, now a hypothesis).

Refuted internal conjecture. That shapes 4211/3221 suffice for all instances meeting the necessary conditions — refuted by explicit dual-verified sole-2222 / sole-3311 / sole-5111 instances; all five shapes are individually necessary.

10Reproducibility — current state and gaps

What exists now: all code, pools, and logs in ~/projects/counterexample-hunt/efx/ (private): verify.py / verify2.py / fast_verify.py, cegar.py, cegar_z3.py, anatomy.py, corridor.py, n5_fourtype.py, chronological results/CAMPAIGN_LOG.md including every correction, and the theory documents. Any instance-level claim is checkable by writing the matrix as JSON {"n":4,"m":8,"values":[[…]]} and running python3 verify2.py that.json (requires only stdlib; the CEGAR stack needs ortools, z3-solver, numpy).

Acknowledged gaps before external circulation (from the referee round, all accepted): no public immutable snapshot/commit or environment lockfile; no run manifest with commands, seeds, versions, hardware, hashes; some final pools lag their terminal iterations, so they are not yet replayable certificates; resumed runs underreport wall time; the two verifiers do not validate the CEGAR master encodings — a second independently implemented master (or solver certificates, e.g. DRAT-style where available) is the right standard for calling these computer-assisted theorems. Until that package exists, §4 should be read as carefully-logged solver runs, not certified theorems.
