\title{\textbf{Complete EFX for Four Additive Agents and Eight Goods}\\
\large A formally audited computer-assisted proof}
\author{Draft for adversarial review}
\date{July 30, 2026}

\begin{abstract}
We prove that every instance of four agents and eight indivisible goods with
nonnegative additive valuations admits a complete allocation that is
envy-free up to any good (EFX). This is the first additive parameter cell
immediately beyond the general $m\le n+3$ existence frontier. The proof is
hybrid. Lean verifies the mathematical reduction from a hypothetical
counterexample to eight exact rational linear-arithmetic systems. A
generator-independent parser audit binds every assertion in those systems to
the Lean terminal-cell specifications. For each system, cvc5 emits a CPC proof
of unsatisfiability and the separate Ethos checker accepts the proof as
\texttt{correct}. The eight proofs contain 13,812,082 proof-step lines and
occupy 1.668 GB. An alternative encoding over all 40,824 labeled onto
allocations independently closes all eight cells. A 124-entry SHA-256 manifest
binds the complete proof package, exact published inputs, tools, audits,
formulas, proofs, Lean build, and source archive. We explain the structural
reduction, the formal-to-computational boundary, and the remaining trust
assumptions. We also report a 130-partition deletion-minimal explanatory core
with its own independently checked CPC proof. This manuscript is a technical
review draft, not a claim of peer review or independently confirmed novelty.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Let $\N$ be a finite set of agents and $\M$ a finite set of indivisible
goods. Agent $i$ has an additive valuation
\[
  v_i(S)=\sum_{g\in S} w_{ig},\qquad w_{ig}\ge0.
\]
A complete allocation $X=(X_i)_{i\in\N}$ partitions all goods. It is EFX if
for every pair of agents $i\ne j$ and every good $g\in X_j$,
\[
  v_i(X_i)\ge v_i(X_j\setminus\{g\}).
\]
The central existence question asks whether a complete EFX allocation always
exists for additive valuations.

Complete EFX is known for three additive agents
\cite{CGM2024}. Mahara proved complete EFX for general monotone valuations
when $m\le n+3$ \cite{Mahara2021}. Thus four agents and seven goods are
covered, while the case of four agents and eight goods is the first
near-diagonal additive cell outside both standard boundaries. Four-agent
partial EFX leaving at most one good unallocated is known
\cite{Berger2022}, but does not settle completeness.

Our main result is the following.

\begin{theorem}[Main theorem]\label{thm:main}
Every nonnegative additive valuation instance with four agents and
eight goods admits a complete EFX allocation.
\end{theorem}

The terminal reduction is carried out for strictly positive weights. To pass
from nonnegative weights, observe that each fixed allocation's EFX region is
closed and there are finitely many allocations. Hence the set of instances
with no EFX allocation is open. Any nonnegative counterexample would therefore
have a sufficiently small strictly positive perturbation that remains a
counterexample. This perturbation reduction and the final nonnegative wrapper
are checked in Lean.

The proof combines four layers:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.1em]
  \item published complete-EFX inputs for four agents and seven goods and for
  three additive agents;
  \item a Lean-checked structural reduction to eight terminal ranking cells;
  \item an independently generated and parser-audited exact linear-arithmetic
  formula for each cell; and
  \item eight cvc5 CPC proofs accepted by the separate Ethos checker.
\end{enumerate}

This organization follows an explicit trust boundary. The mathematical
reduction is not inferred from solver output. Conversely, Lean does not prove
billions of arithmetic proof steps internally. It proves the finite
reduction; exact formula audits connect that reduction to independently
checked arithmetic certificates.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
Besides Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, the development provides:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.8em]
  \item a reverse-parking criterion and a terminating threshold-cycle normal
  form for EFX allocations;
  \item a generic favorite-small-bundle theorem: if every agent receives a
  favorite good and every bundle has size at most two, the allocation is EFX;
  \item a formal construction closing the injective-favorite case whenever
  $n\le m\le2n$;
  \item exact canonical coverage, stabilizer, partition-enumeration, and
  perfect-matching equivalence theorems in Lean;
  \item an independent direct-allocation encoding for all eight cells; and
  \item a deletion-minimal 130-partition core that exposes the geometry of
  one terminal contradiction.
\end{itemize}

\section{Definitions and basic reductions}

\begin{definition}
For an agent $i$ and bundle $S$, define the one-good-removal threshold
\[
  \tau_i(S)=\max_{g\in S} v_i(S\setminus\{g\}),\qquad
  \tau_i(\varnothing)=0.
\]
\end{definition}

An allocation is EFX exactly when
\[
  v_i(X_i)\ge \tau_i(X_j)
  \quad\text{for all }i,j.
\]
For additive valuations,
\[
  \tau_i(S)=v_i(S)-\min_{g\in S}v_i(g).
\]

\subsection{Reverse parking and threshold cycles}

Suppose $X$ is EFX and $f\in X_h$. Removing $f$ can only make the observer
inequalities of $h$ harder; all other agents see a target bundle shrink.
Consequently $f$ is removable from $h$ precisely when
\[
  v_h(X_h)-v_h(f)\ge \max_{j\ne h}\tau_h(X_j).
\]
Choosing a least-valued good in $X_h$ yields the following criterion.

\begin{lemma}[Reverse parking]\label{lem:parking}
An owner $h$ has a removable good if and only if
\[
  \tau_h(X_h)\ge\max_{j\ne h}\tau_h(X_j).
\]
\end{lemma}

If no owner satisfies this inequality, choose for every owner $h$ a strict
threshold-maximizing target $c(h)$. The resulting functional graph contains a
directed cycle. Rotate bundles along that cycle. EFX is preserved, and the
finite potential
\[
  \Phi(X)=\sum_h\tau_h(X_h)
\]
strictly increases. Repeating must terminate.

\begin{theorem}[Threshold-cycle normal form]\label{thm:cycle}
Every complete EFX allocation with nonempty bundles can be transformed by
EFX-preserving bundle rotations into one having an owner-removable good.
\end{theorem}

The theorem is generic over finite agents and goods and nonnegative additive
valuations in the Lean development. Strict positivity forces all four bundles
to be nonempty in the $4\times8$ setting.

\subsection{Normalization and strict rankings}

Multiplying one agent's valuation row by a positive scalar preserves every
EFX comparison. We normalize each row so that
\[
  \sum_{g\in\M}w_{ig}=1.
\]
There are only finitely many complete allocations. If a normalized instance
has no EFX allocation, choose one strict failed inequality for each
allocation. These failures persist in an open neighborhood. A sufficiently
small sum-preserving perturbation therefore makes every row ranking strict
while preserving positivity and nonexistence.

Lean proves this simultaneous perturbation rather than treating genericity as
an informal ``without loss of generality'' step.

\subsection{Favorite small bundles}

\begin{lemma}[Favorite-small-bundle lemma]\label{lem:favorites}
Let $X$ be a complete allocation in a positive additive instance. If every
agent owns one of its favorite goods and every bundle has size at most two,
then $X$ is EFX.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
After removing any good from a target bundle, at most one good remains. The
observer values that good no more than its own favorite, which is contained
in its own bundle.
\end{proof}

The Lean theorem is more general: given an injective favorite map and
$|\M|\le2|\N|$, it constructs the allocation and proves EFX. In particular,
if four agents have four distinct favorite goods among eight goods, give each
agent its favorite and one remaining good.

\section{Reduction to eight terminal cells}

Assume for contradiction that a positive additive $4\times8$ instance has no
complete EFX allocation. Normalize and perturb it as above.

\subsection{Residual cuts from published theorems}

Two structurally easy regions are removed.

\paragraph{Universal minimum.}
If a good is minimum for every agent, delete it, apply Mahara's complete
$4\times7$ theorem, rotate to an unenvied source, and insert the good there.

\paragraph{Peelable agent-good pair.}
If an agent-good pair satisfies the formal peelability inequalities, delete
that agent and good, apply the complete three-agent additive theorem to the
remaining seven goods, and reinsert the pair.

The latter reduction initially exposed a genuine specification error during
audit: the deleted agent had incorrectly been retained as an observer with an
empty bundle. The corrected predicate excludes that observer while retaining
all comparisons needed for reinsertion. The correction and all downstream
theorems are machine-checked.

\subsection{Favorite multiplicities}

By Lemma~\ref{lem:favorites}, favorites cannot be distinct. With strict
rankings, the noninjective favorite multiplicities are, up to relabeling,
\[
  2+1+1,\qquad3+1,\qquad2+2,\qquad4.
\]
Canonical favorite vectors for the first three are
\[
  (0,0,1,2),\qquad(0,0,0,1),\qquad(0,0,1,1).
\]
If all four agents favor good $0$, refine by the multiplicities of their
second favorites:
\[
  1+1+1+1,\qquad4,\qquad3+1,\qquad2+2,\qquad2+1+1.
\]
This gives exactly eight terminal cells.

Only symmetries preserving a fixed ranking prefix are used. Agents with the
same prefix and goods absent from every prescribed prefix may be sorted
lexicographically. Lean proves that every relevant orbit has a representative
satisfying these stabilizer inequalities.

\section{Partitions and perfect matchings}

There are exactly
\[
  S(8,4)=1,701
\]
unlabeled partitions of eight goods into four nonempty bundles. The Lean
development verifies the restricted-growth enumeration and proves that every
onto labeled allocation is obtained from one enumerated partition by a
permutation of the four bundles.

Fix a partition $\mathcal B=(B_0,B_1,B_2,B_3)$. Construct a bipartite graph
between agents and bundles. Put edge $iB_b$ when
\[
  v_i(B_b)\ge \max_{c\ne b}\tau_i(B_c).
\]

\begin{proposition}[Matching equivalence]\label{prop:matching}
The partition $\mathcal B$ supports a complete EFX allocation if and only if
its agent-bundle feasibility graph has a perfect matching.
\end{proposition}

Thus a counterexample must obstruct all $4!=24$ matchings for every one of
the 1,701 partitions.

\section{Exact terminal formulas}

Each terminal formula is quantifier-free rational linear arithmetic. It
contains:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.8em]
  \item 32 strictly positive valuation variables and four row-normalization
  equations;
  \item exact favorite or second-favorite constraints;
  \item the valid cell stabilizers;
  \item exclusions of the universal-minimum and peelable regions;
  \item 27,216 agent-bundle feasibility-edge definitions; and
  \item 40,824 perfect-matching obstruction assertions.
\end{itemize}

The final formulas are generated by a program that does not import the
original encoder, the original partition implementation, or Z3. A separate
parser audit reconstructs the exact assertion semantics, independently
enumerates the 1,701 partitions, checks every edge definition and all 24
matching obstructions per partition, and binds the cell constants to their

\section{Certificate closure}

For every terminal formula:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.1em]
  \item the original exact Z3 computation returns \texttt{unsat};
  \item the archived formula replays in a fresh process;
  \item an isolated rebuild matches the archived formula byte for byte;
  \item the independent generator emits a semantically audited formula;
  \item cvc5 emits a CPC proof; and
  \item Ethos accepts that proof as \texttt{correct}.
\end{enumerate}

The eight CPC proofs total 1,668,457,899 bytes and 13,812,082 proof-step
lines. Formula, proof, tool, all 51 transitive CPC-signature files, paper, and
audit hashes are bound by a 96-entry certificate manifest. An additional
audit matches all 545,360 formula assertions to all 545,360 permanent CPC
assumptions in order, with zero mismatches.

\subsection{Alternative direct encoding}

As a non-load-bearing cross-check, a second formulation directly negates
every labeled onto allocation. There are
\[
  4!\,S(8,4)=40,824
\]
such allocations. This encoding does not use the 1,701-partition
feasibility-graph representation. All eight direct formulas are UNSAT: six
close in Z3, and the two hardest close in cvc5 after an audited identity-only
SMT-LIB syntax normalization.

\subsection{Lean composition and axiom audit}

Lean proves the theorem
\[
\texttt{admitsEFX\_4x8\_of\_standard\_published\_inputs}.
\]
Its permanent axiom audit reports exactly
\[
  [\texttt{propext},\ \texttt{Classical.choice},\ \texttt{Quot.sound}],
\]
with no \texttt{sorryAx} dependency and no native-computation axiom. The exact
Mahara and Chaudhury--Garg--Mehlhorn paper versions and theorem fragments are
hash-pinned; their proofs remain external trusted mathematics rather than
re-formalized Lean proofs.

\subsection{End-to-end package}

The frozen finalizer:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.8em]
  \item verifies every CPC and direct-encoding artifact hash;
  \item reruns the semantic and published-input audits;
  \item checks the Lean source inventory and root imports;
  \item performs a full pinned Lean build and axiom audit; and
  \item creates a deterministic archive containing the transitive proof
  sources.
\end{itemize}
All 124 entries in the final SHA-256 manifest verify. The deterministic
source archive contains 72 files. A clean-source replay script extracts it
into a new temporary project with no prior project build directory and repeats
both audits, the Lean build, and the axiom check; independent-host replay
remains part of the external release gate.

\section{The 130-partition explanatory core}

The favorite-$211$ cell has favorite vector $(0,0,1,2)$. Starting from all
1,701 partition obstructions, assumption-core extraction produced 200
partitions. Deletion shrinking then obtained a 130-partition core.

Every single-partition deletion from the final core is satisfiable, so every
survivor is individually indispensable relative to this core. This is
deletion minimality, not a minimum-cardinality theorem.

The final core has an exact two-piece description:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.1em]
  \item 110 partitions in which shared favorite good $0$ is a singleton,
  favorite goods $0,1,2$ are separated, and no nonfavorite good is a
  singleton; and
  \item 20 pair partitions of shape $2+2+2+2$ in which good $0$ is paired
  with one nonfavorite good.
\end{enumerate}
The reduced formula has a 220,777,714-byte CPC proof containing 2,032,963
proof-step lines; Ethos accepts it as \texttt{correct}.

This geometry suggests an analytic target: prove that either one of the
anchored singleton partitions or one of a small boundary family of anchored
pair partitions has a perfect EFX matching. Ongoing deletion-witness analysis
extracts exact valuations for which each indispensable partition is the sole
EFX-supporting partition within the 130-partition core, counts all support in
the full 1,701-partition universe, and exposes the Hall-deficient agent subsets
in the other 129 core partitions.

\section{Trust boundary and review status}

The internally verified deduction has the form
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{two pinned published EFX theorems}\\
&+\ \text{Lean-checked structural reduction}\\
&+\ \text{independently audited Lean-to-SMT binding}\\
&+\ \text{eight CPC/Ethos UNSAT certificates}\\
&\Longrightarrow\ \text{complete positive additive EFX at }(4,8).
\end{aligned}
\]

The remaining trusted components are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.8em]
  \item the mathematical proofs in the two published input papers;
  \item the Lean kernel, pinned toolchain, and mathlib;
  \item the Ethos checker implementation; and
  \item the parser-based semantic bridge, which is audited software rather
  than a single reflected theorem in Lean's kernel.
\end{itemize}

No independent fair-division or formal-certification expert has yet signed
off. The package has a fresh-source replay on the original host, but not a
second-machine or cross-operating-system replay. Accordingly, the warranted
status is:
\begin{quote}
\textbf{Internally verified hybrid computer-assisted theorem; external
review and novelty confirmation pending.}
\end{quote}

\section{Research directions}

Three directions are immediate.

\paragraph{Short analytic contradiction.}
Use the deletion witnesses to identify repeated Hall-deficient agent sets and
translate their missing edges into threshold inequalities. Search for small
Farkas combinations that explain the 130-way disjunction.

\paragraph{Next parameter cells.}
The universal-minimum and peelable reductions make $(4,9)$ and $(5,9)$ the
natural successors. At $(5,9)$, the generic injective-favorite theorem still
closes the distinct-favorite region because $9\le2\cdot5$. Hall-selector
encodings may avoid enumerating all $5!=120$ matchings per partition.

\paragraph{Counting.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} proves at least one EFX allocation at $(4,8)$. The
additive counting conjecture asks for at least four. Threshold-cycle
rotations and multiplicities of perfect matchings may help strengthen
existence into a counting statement.
